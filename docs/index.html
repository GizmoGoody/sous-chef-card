Grill Card: mockups

A Home Assistant card for meat probes, built first for ThermoWorks RFX probes on the ThermoWorks Cloud integration. It is Home Assistant's own tile card, wrapped. Everything a tile card does works the same way, with the same editor. The card adds a probe's cook preset, its progress to the target, and the reading from each sensor in the probe. These features are added like any other tile feature. Three layouts are shown so we can choose one.

Cook presets

  
These are built into the card. They are the values from your Grill Probe Preset Update automation, so that automation and the eight preset helpers are no longer needed. Choosing a type and a doneness in the Cook feature writes the target temperature to the probe's target entity. A card can change or add presets with the presets option. Manual keeps whatever target you set.

  


  
Notes

  
    
    
  • Finding the probe's sensorsThe ThermoWorks Cloud integration does not give its sensors translation keys. Their names come from the ThermoWorks account, such as "Unnamed channel (ch. 1)". So the card finds them by integration, device and device class. Channel 0 is the probe's reading, and channels 1 to 4 are its four sensors. The battery and signal sensors are found by their own device classes. The alarm thresholds are temperature sensors too. The card leaves them out, and while writing the code I will find the most reliable way to recognize them: an entity category if the integration sets one, otherwise their names. A probe from another integration works through the same device class lookup. Any of these can be overridden in the editor.
    • 
    
  • The reading is channel 0The RFX MEAT has four sensors along the shaft, below the immersion line. Its default channel, channel 0, is the lowest of the four, as the ThermoWorks app shows it. So channel 0 is the tile's entity and its state is the reading, with no helper and no trick. The Min helpers are no longer needed, and the card does not use the Max and Median helpers. Channels 1 and 4 are the two ends of the probe; your cook history shows the four in order every time. The editor's "Sensor at the tip" option puts the Channels feature in order from the tip.
    • 
    
  • Rate and time leftTime left is (target − reading) ÷ rate. The rate comes from a Derivative helper when you choose one, as your current setup has. Without one, the card hides time left and the stall pattern. The card does not work out a rate from history itself, because a Derivative helper is how Home Assistant does that.
    • 
    
  • In use, and always showA probe counts as in use while its channels report a temperature. A probe that is not in use collapses to its title row on its own card (layout A) and is hidden from the session card (layout B). The "Always show" option in the editor replaces the Active Override helpers and the row of colored dots.
    • 
    
  • Where "Beef, Medium Rare" is kept to decideThe target entity holds only a number, such as 145. The card cannot work out the food from the number, because 145 °F is Beef Medium, Pork Medium and Salmon. So the chosen type and doneness need an entity of their own, and that entity is also what lets the choice show on every device.
    • 
    
  • Both layouts, one cardThere is one card type. What it shows depends on its entity. With a probe's reading as its entity, it is a probe card (layout A). With any other entity, such as the grill ambient sensor, it is a grill card, and you add one Probe feature for each probe (layout B). The Probe feature is drawn by the same code as the probe card's Progress, Cook and Channels features, so the two layouts always match. Use either one, or both on the same dashboard (C).
    • 
    
  • What stays in Home AssistantAlerts ("Red is done", "Grill below 225 °F") stay automations, and the repository will offer a blueprint for them. The card does not notify.
    • 
    
  • RepositoryOne plain JavaScript file with no build step and no libraries, like the xTool card: grill-card.js, hacs.json, a README, this page under docs/ for GitHub Pages, and the xTool card's headless browser tests and HACS check in GitHub Actions.
    • 
  

  
Mockup data is invented to show each state. Temperatures are in °F, as your probes report them. Icons are Material Design Icons, the set Home Assistant uses.
